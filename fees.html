Transaction Cost Reference

Every cost that isn't already inside the quoted odds. Rates verified August 2026.
Verify these against your own accounts before trusting them. Commission varies by tier, region, promotion and account age. A rate that's wrong by 2 percentage points is enough to turn a profitable arb strategy into a losing one.
Understanding the prediction-market formula
Prediction markets price in contracts, not odds. C = number of contracts. P = price per contract in dollars, which is the implied probability — a contract at $0.40 implies 40% and pays $1.00 if it happens.

fee = coeff × C × P × (1 − P)

Because of the P × (1 − P) term the fee peaks at even money and shrinks toward both extremes. A 50/50 contract is the most expensive thing on the board; a 5¢ longshot or 95¢ near-lock is cheap. Arbs and +EV bets cluster on competitive matchups, so they sit on the expensive part of that curve.

In stake terms, since P = 1/odds and C = stake/P, this collapses to:
fee = coeff × stake × (odds − 1) / odds

Exchanges EX

Commission on net winnings, charged only when you win. Nothing on a loss. Betfair charges per market, so a back and lay that net to zero pay nothing.

Prediction markets PM

Fee charged on entry — win or lose. It is sunk the moment the trade fills, unlike exchange commission.
PlatformRateNotes
Kalshi0.07 × C × P × (1−P)Taker fee, rounded up to the next cent. Maximum ~1.75% at P = 0.50. Resting limit orders (maker) are substantially cheaper or free. No settlement fee, no membership fee. Card deposits up to 2%; bank transfers free.
Polymarket0.05 × C × P × (1−P)Sports coefficient as of July 2026, raised from 0.03. Makers pay nothing. Geopolitics markets remain fee-free. The US regulated exchange uses a flat 0.30% taker with a 0.20% maker rebate instead. On-chain gas costs apply.

Sportsbooks SB

No separate fee at any of these. Their entire revenue is the overround baked into the odds — typically 5–8%, and around 2–3% at Pinnacle. Shopping for the best price is shopping for the lowest fee.

Costs that don't attach to a single bet

These sit outside the per-trade model but hit a real account, and two of them dwarf everything above.
CostDetail
Betfair Expert FeeReplaced the Premium Charge on 6 January 2025. Tiered on rolling 52-week gross profit: 0% below £25k, 20% from £25k–£100k, 40% above £100k, applied weekly and on top of standard commission. This is the single largest cost facing a consistently profitable exchange bettor, and it scales precisely with success.
Account limiting / closureNot a fee, but in practice the dominant real-world cost. Sportsbooks restrict stake sizes or close accounts that win consistently, frequently within weeks of detecting arbitrage patterns. Exchanges do not limit — they earn commission either way. Pinnacle notably does not limit. This ends more arbitrage operations than commission ever does.
Currency conversion2–4% typical where account currency differs from market currency. Relevant for Kalshi and Polymarket (USD-denominated) funded from a GBP bankroll — this routinely exceeds the trading fee itself and is easy to overlook.
Withdrawal feesUsually £0 by bank transfer at UK books. Kalshi free by bank, card deposits up to 2%. Polymarket incurs on-chain gas costs.
Bet cancellation / palpable errorBooks void bets priced in obvious error. An arb that looks too good to be true is the likeliest candidate to be voided — leaving you holding one unhedged leg with real directional risk.
Dormancy feesSome operators charge monthly after 12+ months of inactivity.
SlippageNot charged by anyone, but a real cost. Displayed top-of-book prices rarely fill in full size on thinner exchange markets, so realised odds are typically worse than quoted. Modelled as a configurable haircut in the analyser.
